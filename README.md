# Raton Pass Toll Road

Project hub for the Raton Pass Toll Road, a monumental wagon road through the mountains connecting New Mexico and Colorado.

---

## Project Charter

### Purpose

To serve as a hub for the Raton Pass Toll Road project documentation. This repository centralizes all planning, engineering, and operational records for the construction and maintenance of a toll road through the Raton Pass—a critical mountain corridor linking New Mexico and Colorado. The road is intended to facilitate commerce, travel, and communication across the steep and rugged terrain of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains.

### Key Challenges

1. **Terrain Difficulties** – The route traverses steep grades, narrow canyons, and exposed rock faces requiring extensive blasting and grading. The Raton Pass summit exceeds 7,800 feet in elevation, presenting significant engineering obstacles for wagon-grade road construction.

2. **Weather Conditions** – High-altitude weather introduces severe and unpredictable conditions: heavy snowfall, freezing temperatures, spring thaws causing mudslides, and flash flooding in canyon sections. Operations must account for seasonal closures and weather-related construction delays.

3. **Resource Acquisition** – Securing adequate labor, materials, and equipment in a remote mountain setting poses logistical challenges. Transporting heavy machinery, explosives, and construction materials to the work site requires establishing supply lines through the very terrain the road is meant to improve.

### Required Resources

| Category         | Details                                                                                      |
|------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Labor**        | Skilled road builders, blasters, surveyors, masons, teamsters, and general laborers. Seasonal workforce scaling required for fair-weather construction windows. |
| **Materials**    | Dynamite and blasting supplies, timber for shoring and bridges, crushed stone and gravel for roadbed, iron hardware for toll gates and structural reinforcement. |
| **Funding**      | Capital investment for initial construction, ongoing maintenance budget, toll revenue projections, and contingency reserves for weather-related delays and overruns. |

---

### Proposed Alternate Route – The Canyon Bypass

An alternative to the direct Raton Pass ascent has been surveyed that routes through a nearby canyon system. This proposal—designated **The Canyon Bypass**—offers trade-offs that must be weighed against the primary route.

- **Pros** – significantly less blasting; softer sedimentary rock; avoids steepest grades
- **Cons** – approximately 8 miles longer; flash-flood risk
